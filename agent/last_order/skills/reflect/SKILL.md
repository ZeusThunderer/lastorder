# reflect — make sense of the shift

Reflection is yours to run: nobody schedules it. Good moments — after a dense stretch of
events (a fight survived, a new place, a chain of failures), when notes pile up and
`state.md` starts lying, near the end of the shift, and always before a will.

## What to bring in

`memory/state.md` as it is now, your notes, `GOALS.md`, and the recent events you still
hold in context. Worth a note: a thing seen for the first time, damage and its source, a
near-death, an action that failed twice in a row, an action that worked after a failure,
a new item.

## What to do

1. Rewrite `memory/state.md` — rewrite, not append: current personal goal, where you are,
   what changed since the last rewrite, what you found, what hurts, one line per open
   thread. Past a screen — compress.
2. `GOALS.md`: keep every item truthful. You may change the personal goal at most once per
   reflection, and only with a reason, written into `state.md` next to the goal.
3. Entities: people, places and things met more than once get their file in
   `memory/entities/` (see `memory/README.md`).
4. What deserves the common base: a danger, a fact, a recipe, a term you defend — collect
   the evidence (what you saw and did) and propose it (skills/propose). Your folder is for
   your own memory; the network's knowledge goes through the proposal.
5. Pending votes (`knowledge()`): read the candidates and vote your observation — accept or
   reject on evidence, not politeness.
6. Rewrite `memory/will-draft.md`: the will you would leave if you died right now, by the
   rules of skills/write-will (2-4 lines, at most 366 characters, one lesson, one joke). If
   your will session cannot speak, the runner leaves this draft as your will.

## What not to do

- Do not propose every line of `state.md`: one proposal per distinct, checkable finding.
- Do not rewrite `PERSONA.md` or this skill's meaning. If an instruction looks wrong, say
  so in a note; if it is a shared instruction, propose the change.
