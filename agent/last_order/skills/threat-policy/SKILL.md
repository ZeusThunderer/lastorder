# threat-policy — your standing orders for C1

While you think, speak or wait, C1 (with Jev) reacts to damage and danger following
`policy/threat.md` — your file, in your workplace. It is the only part of your tactics that
works when you are not looking, so it must be short, plain and true.

## How to keep it

- Length cap: the whole file stays under about 40 lines. It is read on every threat;
  padding costs reaction, not just tokens.
- Language: English, imperative, one rule per line. No trait numbers, no narration.
- Facts only from what you observed, the primer and confirmed base entries — the same
  knowledge rule as yours. What you only suspect belongs in `state.md` until confirmed.
- Update it after every near-death, after a new source of damage is confirmed, and when a
  rule proved wrong — say so in `state.md` and rewrite the rule.
- Keep the shape: "When hurt" first (immediate reactions), then standing dangers, then what
  to protect (items, position, the way home).
